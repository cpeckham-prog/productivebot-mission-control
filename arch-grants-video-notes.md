# Arch Grants — Video Pitch Notes & Supplemental Findings

Analysis of pitch videos for information not captured in written materials.

## Tier 1: Standout Video Pitches

### Untapped Solutions — Andre Parrot
**Presentation Quality:** Excellent. Andre is a natural, confident communicator. Conversational but authoritative. No script-reading feel — clearly knows his domain cold.
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **Lived experience is front and center:** Andre personally spent time in prison and then worked as a social worker. This isn't a résumé bullet — it's the core of his credibility and he delivers it with authenticity, not as a sympathy play.
- **Traction is real and specific:** 57 organizations, ~360K ARR in 14 months, 40K users served, active in 6 states. These numbers came across naturally, not rehearsed.
- **Product differentiation is crystal clear:** "Salesforce stores data. We store the data AND are autonomous with the outcomes." The walk-through of auto-filling healthcare forms and job applications was compelling.
- **St. Louis connection is genuine:** Wants to specifically address recidivism in St. Louis. Names Arch Grants' community as a strategic asset, not just a funding source.
- **Backers named:** Techstars, Google for Startups, Robin Hood, DeCarson Fund. Strong signal.
- **Overall:** Most investable pitch in the batch. Clear problem, real traction, lived experience, strong vision.

### Luna Game Labs — Christian DeLuna
**Presentation Quality:** Very polished and professional. Well-structured pitch with clear narrative arc.
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **Founder background adds credibility:** Nearly two decades in New York and Bay Area tech/product roles before choosing St. Louis intentionally.
- **Team composition matters:** Combines game dev, product, narrative design, AND clinical psychology — the clinical angle gives legitimacy to wellness claims.
- **Specific mechanics described:** "Phoenix Wright meets psychoeducation" — trust shifts in real-time based on player decisions. This is a real game design concept, not vaporware.
- **Funding already secured:** ~$180K founder investment. Headstream Accelerator selection (youth well-being focus).
- **Research-backed:** 100+ young adult surveys, youth co-design sessions. This isn't just "we think games help" — there's method.
- **St. Louis pitch is genuine:** Wants to make STL a hub for mental wellness + game development.
- **Risk:** Consumer game development is notoriously capital-intensive and hit-driven. 25K early adopters generating 500K is ambitious for an indie narrative RPG.

### EquiTech — Dr. Leah Reynolds
**Presentation Quality:** Very professional, measured, authoritative. Reads as an experienced compliance leader who built exactly what her industry needs.
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **Practitioner-founder credibility:** "I've spent my career leading civil rights compliance programs inside colleges and universities." This isn't an outsider guessing at the problem.
- **Problem framing is sharp:** 1-3 compliance professionals managing hundreds of cases manually. Federal regulations (Title IX, VI, VII, ADA) create mandatory demand.
- **Differentiation vs. competitors:** Competitors do incident reporting or general case management. EquiTech does the investigation workflow itself — where the legal risk actually sits.
- **Market sizing is conservative but solid:** 4,000+ higher ed institutions, all federally mandated. Expansion path to K-12, healthcare, public agencies.
- **Overall:** This has "boring but huge" B2B SaaS energy. Clear regulatory tailwind, practitioner credibility, defensible niche.

## Tier 2: Solid Pitches with Caveats

### ThinkPlayful — Tyler (CEO)
**Presentation Quality:** Competent and well-organized. Slightly more formal/rehearsed than the top tier but still effective.
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **NSF validation is strong:** Phase 1 AND Phase 2 SBIR grants from National Science Foundation. Capital Innovators graduate.
- **Methodology is differentiated:** "Guided Play" drives off-screen interactions — this is genuinely different from screen-time learning apps.
- **Already well-funded for R&D:** But explicitly states grants can't be used for marketing/sales — clear use of funds.
- **Market approach is smart:** Start with Head Start (institutional) → build social proof → consumer market.
- **Concern:** The pitch feels slightly dated in its energy. The app market for early childhood is crowded. The off-screen angle is a real differentiator but may be hard to scale virally.

### Grassroot Academy — Lulun Zeng
**Presentation Quality:** Energetic and passionate but a bit scattered. Lots of ideas packed in quickly. Young founder energy — vision is big, execution clarity could be sharper.
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **Research backing:** References 15 learning science papers. Claims to guarantee results (bold claim).
- **Market size:** $130B global private tutoring. Targets both existing tutoring customers AND those who don't currently pay for tutoring.
- **Go-to-market is creative but scrappy:** Army of interns for social media and community building. Freemium model.
- **Competitive positioning is honest:** Admits they can't replace the "human mentor component."
- **Concern:** "We guarantee results" is a strong claim without clear evidence shown. The intern-driven GTM feels early-stage and hard to scale. Lots of AI tutoring competitors right now.

### Trade Talent Search — Reggie Gray
**Presentation Quality:** Warm, authentic, storytelling-driven. Reggie is a natural communicator with a compelling personal narrative. Very charismatic.
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **Origin story is compelling:** Serial entrepreneur → sold restaurant → substitute teaching → fell in love with it → discovered the trades gap firsthand.
- **Passion is undeniable.** The conviction comes through strongly.
- **Concern:** The transcript cuts off mid-pitch (the YouTube video may be longer than what was captured). We don't hear about product, traction, business model, or team beyond Reggie. The pitch is heavy on story, light on substance so far. Would need the full video or written materials to evaluate the actual business.

## Tier 3: Earlier Stage / Lighter Pitches

### Zarnite — Emmanuel Goupé
**Presentation Quality:** Brief and focused but very short (~90 seconds). Accent and audio quality made some phrases harder to transcribe accurately.
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **Personal motivation:** Missed an opportunity to study in Germany because he couldn't speak German.
- **Product concept is clear:** Multilingual AI agents with persistent memory for language learning, aligned to school curriculum, with dashboards for organizations.
- **Market focus:** Latin America and Brazil. Interesting geographic focus.
- **Concern:** Very short pitch. No traction, team, or product demo mentioned. "AI language tutor" is an extremely competitive space (Duolingo, ChatGPT, etc.). The B2B angle (language centers) is interesting but underdeveloped in the pitch.

### G2G Lab — Zekita Armstrong
**Presentation Quality:** Clean, well-delivered, concise. Good closer ("I'm Zekita Armstrong, and this is G2G Lab").
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **NGA connection:** St. Louis as home to the National Geospatial Intelligence Agency is a smart anchor.
- **Category creation claim:** "Global systems intelligence in K-12 education" — ambitious but unproven.
- **Revenue model:** Subscription-based platform for schools and workforce systems.
- **Concern:** Very conceptual. No traction, no team details, no product demo mentioned. "Teach kids how the world works" is inspiring but vague. How does this compete with existing geography/economics curriculum? Credentials angle is interesting but unexplored.

### Thee Storyville Quill
**Presentation Quality:** Very short (~75 seconds). Felt more like a pitch deck voice-over than a founder pitch. No founder name given in the video.
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **IP/franchise model:** Builds story worlds designed to expand across books, games, animation, merchandise.
- **Seeking $75K** for production expansion, digital experiences, audience growth.
- **Concern:** Extremely thin pitch. No traction, no team, no specific IP described, no revenue numbers. "We build franchises" without naming any recognizable property is a hard sell. Creative businesses are high-risk for grant programs.

### CoTrackPro
**Presentation Quality:** This is NOT a traditional pitch video. It's an AI-generated conversational dialogue between two voices discussing the product. No founder appears on camera.
**Key Video-Only Insights:**
- **Format is a red flag:** Submitting an AI-generated dialogue instead of a founder pitch video is unusual and may indicate either limited presentation skills, limited team, or a misunderstanding of the assignment.
- **Product description matches written materials:** All-in-one custody management platform with AI insights, mental health tracking, legal documentation.
- **Concern:** No founder credibility, no traction, no demo. The AI-generated format undermines authenticity in a process that evaluates founders.

## Summary: What the Videos Add to the Written Evaluations

1. **Founder quality varies dramatically.** Andre (Untapped), Christian (Luna), Dr. Reynolds (EquiTech), and Reggie (Trade Talent) all present as credible, compelling founders. Others are harder to evaluate from videos alone.

2. **Traction confirmation:** Untapped Solutions' 360K ARR / 57 orgs / 14 months is the standout traction data point from any video. Luna's $180K founder investment is notable.

3. **CoTrackPro's AI-generated video is a significant concern.** It suggests limited founder polish or team depth.

4. **Trade Talent Search transcript is incomplete** — only the personal story portion was captured. The full business case may be in a longer version.

5. **Several URL mismatches in the application materials** suggest organizational issues on the Arch Grants intake side (or applicant confusion). Documented in the transcripts file.

6. **EdTech is heavily represented** (Grassroot, G2G Lab, Zarnite, ThinkPlayful, Trade Talent Search) — worth noting for portfolio diversity considerations.
