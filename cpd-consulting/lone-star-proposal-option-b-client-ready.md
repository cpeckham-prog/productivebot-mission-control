## Executive Summary

Lone Star Markets has the right kind of problem: strong demand, a compelling offer, and enough market pull that the current commercial motion is starting to outgrow the way it was built.

The company is not struggling because the market does not want the product. The opposite appears true. Jake is managing 50+ opportunities shortly after joining, April produced 37 signed buildings, and the no-cost amenity model gives Lone Star a strong entry point with multifamily operators.

The constraint now is operational. Lone Star is moving from founder-led sales into a repeatable revenue motion. That requires more than a CRM decision. It requires a clear process for qualifying opportunities, reaching the real decision makers, creating urgency without discounting, converting verbal commitments into signed agreements, and giving leadership reliable visibility into what will close and why.

CPD Operating Group recommends a focused 90-day Embedded GTM Acceleration Sprint. The purpose is to help Lone Star install the commercial operating system needed for its next stage of growth: practical pipeline discipline, sharper buyer messaging, stronger deal control, a fit-for-purpose CRM blueprint, and a weekly cadence that helps the team move the right opportunities faster.

---

## Current Situation

Based on the discussion to date, Lone Star appears to have five high-leverage opportunities.

### 1. Demand is strong, but pipeline control is still early
Jake is already carrying significant opportunity volume. Without consistent stages, next-step discipline, deal scoring, and source/stage reporting, strong activity can mask leakage and make it harder to know which opportunities deserve the most attention.

### 2. Founder-led selling needs to become transferable
Founder-led sales works because founders bring urgency, context, and authority into every conversation. As Lone Star shifts more responsibility to Jake and future sales hires, that judgment needs to be converted into a repeatable process.

### 3. Verbal commitments are not consistently becoming signed agreements
The most immediate value is likely late in the funnel. If prospects verbally agree but signatures lag, the issue is usually not interest. It is deal control: unclear signer path, weak mutual timeline, limited consequence for delay, or insufficient ownership-level engagement.

### 4. Property managers can become both champions and blockers
On-site property managers matter, but they are not always the economic or portfolio decision maker. Lone Star needs a practical way to use PM enthusiasm to reach regional managers, VPs, and ownership without damaging the relationship with the site-level champion.

### 5. The no-cost model reduces friction, but can reduce urgency
“No budget required” is a powerful offer. It also creates a risk: prospects may treat the decision as easy to say yes to but easy to delay. Lone Star needs urgency mechanisms based on resident experience, leasing season timing, operational capacity, install windows, and portfolio standardization rather than discounting.

## Recommended Engagement: Embedded GTM Acceleration Sprint

### Objective

Build and install a practical revenue operating system that helps Lone Star:

- Move qualified deals from interest to signed agreement faster
- Reduce time spent chasing unclear verbal commitments
- Improve access to real decision makers
- Create urgency without discounting or artificial pressure
- Select and structure the right CRM for how the business actually sells
- Give leadership a clearer view of pipeline, forecast, and operational capacity

This is designed as an execution-oriented engagement, not a strategy memo. The work will produce usable tools, templates, cadence, and operating discipline that the team can keep using after the sprint.

---

## 90-Day Workplan

### Phase 1: Diagnose and Triage — Weeks 1-2

**Focus:** Understand the current pipeline, isolate bottlenecks, and create immediate movement in stuck opportunities.

Key activities:
- Review active pipeline, current tracking method, sample contracts, sales materials, and follow-up history
- Segment opportunities by stage, unit count, management company, signer access, close likelihood, and next action
- Identify stalled verbal commitments and high-value accounts that need targeted intervention
- Establish baseline metrics: stage duration, verbal-to-signed conversion, time since last touch, expected close date, and signer status
- Define the minimum viable pipeline fields needed for reliable inspection

Outputs:
- Pipeline diagnostic and bottleneck summary
- Late-stage deal triage list
- Priority opportunity scorecard
- Immediate action plan for stalled deals

### Phase 2: Build the Sales Operating System — Weeks 3-6

**Focus:** Convert founder intuition into a clear, repeatable sales process.

Key activities:
- Define clean deal stages from inbound lead through signed agreement
- Create stage exit criteria so the team knows what must be true before a deal advances
- Build a mutual action plan template that turns verbal yes into a dated path to signature
- Create talk tracks and email templates for PMs, regional leaders, and ownership
- Develop a decision-chain map for common customer types
- Clarify when Harrison/founder involvement should be used to unblock strategic accounts

Outputs:
- Sales process map
- Stage definitions and exit criteria
- Mutual action plan template
- Follow-up, signer-access, and post-rendering email templates
- PM/regional/ownership messaging framework

### Phase 3: CRM and Revenue Operations Blueprint — Weeks 5-8

**Focus:** Choose and design the system before buying or overbuilding the tool.

Key activities:
- Assess HubSpot, Pipedrive, or other lightweight CRM options against Lone Star’s actual workflow
- Recommend the best-fit CRM path based on usability, automation, reporting, Gmail/calendar fit, cost, and future scale
- Define required properties, pipeline stages, dashboards, automations, and reporting views
- Design leadership reporting around what Harrison needs to see weekly
- Build an implementation plan that avoids unnecessary complexity

Outputs:
- CRM recommendation
- Pipeline/stage architecture
- Required fields and dashboard blueprint
- Automation priorities
- CRM implementation roadmap

### Phase 4: Install Cadence and Scale Plan — Weeks 7-12

**Focus:** Make the new process stick and translate it into weekly operating discipline.

Key activities:
- Run weekly pipeline reviews with Jake
- Hold biweekly leadership sessions with Harrison/Jake around bottlenecks, forecast, and operating decisions
- Coach Jake on late-stage deal control, stakeholder access, and prioritization
- Align sales commitments with installation capacity and operational readiness
- Build the next-phase roadmap for CRM rollout, hiring, portfolio-account strategy, and sales playbook development

Outputs:
- Weekly pipeline review format
- Leadership reporting template
- Deal review / coaching rhythm
- 90-day findings readout
- Next-phase GTM roadmap
