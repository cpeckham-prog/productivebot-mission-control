## Observed Challenges

Based on the current context, Lone Star’s challenges appear to be growth-stage operating issues rather than demand issues.

### 1. Strong inbound volume, limited revenue infrastructure
Jake is already managing 50+ opportunities shortly after joining. Without a clean CRM, defined stages, source tracking, next-step discipline, and prioritization rules, high volume can create hidden leakage.

### 2. Founder-led sales transitioning to a repeatable motion
Founder-led selling often works because founders carry urgency, context, and authority into every conversation. As Jake takes on more of the commercial load, the process needs to become explicit: what qualifies an opportunity, who must be involved, what happens after the site visit, and how a verbal commit becomes a signed agreement.

### 3. Deals stalling after verbal commitment
The biggest near-term value is likely in the late-stage pipeline. If agreements are verbally approved but contracts require repeated follow-up, the issue may be unclear ownership, weak mutual timelines, lack of signer engagement, or insufficient consequence for delay.

### 4. Property managers can gatekeep ownership
PMs are often important champions, but they may not be the final signer or budget/portfolio authority. Lone Star needs a practical way to earn access to regional leaders, VPs, and ownership without alienating PM relationships.

### 5. The no-cost model reduces friction but can reduce urgency
“No cost” is a powerful offer, but it can also make the decision feel non-urgent. Lone Star needs urgency mechanisms that do not rely on discounting: installation capacity, resident experience timing, leasing seasonality, competitive amenity positioning, implementation slots, portfolio standardization, and mutual action plans.

## Recommended Engagement: 90-Day GTM Acceleration Sprint

### Objective
Build a practical, repeatable GTM operating system that improves deal velocity, reduces contract drag, and gives leadership clearer visibility into what is working.

### Workstream 1: Pipeline Audit and Deal Triage
- Review active opportunities and categorize by stage, likelihood, signer access, unit count, management company, and next action.
- Identify late-stage stalled deals that can be advanced with targeted intervention.
- Define a simple qualification and prioritization model so Jake spends time on the highest-value opportunities.
- Establish baseline metrics: current cycle time, time in stage, verbal-to-signed conversion, follow-up volume, and install pipeline.

### Workstream 2: Sales Process and Stage Design
- Define a clear sales process from inbound lead to signed agreement.
- Create stage exit criteria so the team knows what must be true before a deal advances.
- Add structure around site visits, rendering review, signer confirmation, contract delivery, and close plan.
- Build a mutual action plan template for prospects so “verbal yes” turns into a dated path to signature.

### Workstream 3: Urgency and Buyer Messaging
- Refine the commercial narrative around why now, not just why Lone Star.
- Create talk tracks for PMs, regional managers, and ownership.
- Develop language that frames Lone Star as a resident experience, leasing, and retention amenity rather than a nice-to-have snack market.
- Introduce non-discount urgency tools: installation windows, implementation readiness, leasing season timing, limited operational capacity, and portfolio rollout planning.

### Workstream 4: Multi-Threading and Decision-Maker Access
- Build a decision-chain map for common customer types.
- Create scripts and email templates for asking PM champions to involve regional or ownership stakeholders.
- Develop a portfolio-account strategy for management companies with multiple eligible buildings.
- Identify when founder involvement should be used selectively to unblock high-value accounts.

### Workstream 5: CRM and Revenue Operations Blueprint
- Assess current tools and workflow.
- Recommend the right CRM path, likely a lightweight HubSpot or Pipedrive setup depending on cost, usability, automation needs, and future operating model.
- Define required fields, pipeline stages, dashboards, automations, and reporting.
- Deliver an implementation roadmap that avoids overbuilding before the team needs it.

### Workstream 6: Operating Cadence and Coaching
- Install a weekly pipeline review rhythm.
- Coach Jake on late-stage deal control, stakeholder access, and prioritization.
- Create a simple leadership view for Harrison: what is closing, what is stuck, why it is stuck, and what decisions are needed.
- Align sales commitments with operational capacity so the team can scale responsibly.
