## Executive Summary

Lone Star Markets appears to be at the stage every strong founder-led company eventually reaches: demand is real, the offer is working, and the business now needs more commercial discipline so growth does not become harder than it needs to be.

The issue does not appear to be market interest. Lone Star has meaningful lead volume, a compelling no-cost amenity model, and clear traction with multifamily properties. The bigger opportunity is turning that traction into a more controlled path from interest to signed agreement.

The questions that matter most now are practical:

- Which opportunities deserve the most attention?
- Where are deals actually getting stuck?
- How can Lone Star create urgency without using price as a lever?
- How can the team reach the real decision maker earlier without damaging the property-manager relationship?
- What should the CRM and sales process look like before the company adds more sales capacity?
- What does Harrison need to see every week to know whether the revenue engine is healthy?

CPD Operating Group recommends a focused 90-day founder-level advisory engagement. The goal is not to promise a full revenue transformation in three months. The goal is to help Harrison sharpen the commercial system, identify the highest-leverage bottlenecks, create a practical operating blueprint, and install enough weekly discipline that Lone Star can make better decisions as it scales.

This is meant to be useful, executive-level, and realistic: hands-on enough to create movement, but scoped tightly enough to avoid overbuilding or pulling the team away from selling.

## Why This Matters Now

Lone Star has a strong offer: a resident amenity that can improve convenience, leasing differentiation, and property experience without requiring upfront capital from the building.

That no-cost model is a major advantage. It also creates a predictable challenge. Because there is no budget event, prospects can agree in principle without feeling urgency to complete the decision. Deals can drift after verbal approval, especially when the actual signer sits above the site-level champion.

For a company with strong demand, the hidden cost of this friction is significant:

- time spent chasing contracts instead of advancing better opportunities
- weak visibility into which deals are real, stalled, or at risk
- delayed installations and less predictable operational planning
- slower learning about which property profiles, management groups, and buyer paths convert best
- increased dependence on founder intuition instead of a repeatable commercial rhythm

The goal is not to make the sales process complicated. It is to make it visible, repeatable, and easier to manage.

---

## Current Read

Based on the discussion and materials reviewed, Lone Star appears to have five high-leverage commercial needs.

### 1. Better pipeline inspection

With strong lead volume and a lean team, the company needs a simple way to separate high-probability, high-value opportunities from deals that are consuming attention without a clear path to signature.

### 2. Clearer decision-maker strategy

Property managers are important champions, but they are not always the final economic or portfolio decision maker. Lone Star needs a repeatable way to identify the signer path earlier and earn access to regional, VP, or ownership-level stakeholders when needed.

### 3. Urgency without discounting

Because Lone Star is not selling a priced product in the traditional sense, urgency has to come from operational timing, leasing priorities, resident experience, installation capacity, competitive differentiation, and clear mutual next steps — not from price concessions.

### 4. A CRM decision based on the actual sales motion

The CRM should support how Lone Star sells, not force the company into unnecessary complexity. Before selecting or configuring a tool, the company needs clarity on stages, fields, reporting, follow-up triggers, and leadership visibility.

### 5. A founder-level operating cadence

Harrison should not have to inspect every deal manually. But at this stage, he does need a reliable weekly view of what is moving, what is stuck, what decisions are needed, and where the commercial motion is improving or leaking.

## 90-Day Scope

### Phase 1: Commercial Diagnostic & Priority Alignment  
**Weeks 1-3**

**Purpose:** Establish a clear picture of the current sales motion and identify the few constraints that matter most.

Key activities:

- review the current pipeline, sales process, materials, follow-up patterns, and available deal history
- interview Harrison and relevant team members to understand the founder-led motion, buyer paths, and growth goals
- segment opportunities by property type, unit count, management company, stage, decision-maker access, and close risk
- identify the highest-value points of leakage from lead through signed agreement
- define the minimum pipeline visibility Harrison needs weekly

Outputs:

- commercial diagnostic summary
- priority bottleneck map
- executive pipeline visibility requirements
- recommended focus areas for the remainder of the engagement

### Phase 2: Revenue Operating Blueprint  
**Weeks 4-7**

**Purpose:** Define the practical commercial system Lone Star needs before it adds more tools, process, or sales capacity.

Key activities:

- define a simple stage model from initial interest to signed agreement
- establish stage exit criteria and next-step requirements
- create a practical approach for verbal commits, signer access, and stalled agreements
- develop a decision-maker mapping framework for property managers, regional leaders, VPs, and ownership
- define urgency levers that fit Lone Star’s no-cost amenity model
- evaluate lightweight CRM options against the actual sales motion

Outputs:

- sales process map
- stage definitions and qualification rules
- verbal-to-signed deal control framework
- stakeholder / signer-path framework
- CRM recommendation and implementation blueprint
- leadership dashboard requirements

### Phase 3: Advisory Cadence & Execution Support  
**Weeks 8-12**

**Purpose:** Pressure-test the blueprint, refine the operating cadence, and decide what should happen next.

Key activities:

- establish a weekly executive pipeline review format
- review priority deals and identify patterns in stalled opportunities
- refine messaging and follow-up approaches for high-leverage scenarios
- advise on CRM rollout sequencing and avoid unnecessary overbuild
- clarify the next phase: sales hire profile, CRM implementation, portfolio-account strategy, or continued fractional support

Outputs:

- weekly executive pipeline review format
- priority-deal review framework
- CRM rollout sequence
- 90-day findings and recommendations readout
- next-phase commercial roadmap

The engagement is designed to create consistent executive-level progress without overwhelming Lone Star’s operating rhythm.

Standard practice is for the client to cover reasonable business travel expenses, including airfare or mileage, lodging, ground transportation, parking, and meals/incidental expenses. Meals and incidentals can be handled either by receipt-based reimbursement or by a standard daily per diem. For planning purposes, a per diem aligned with prevailing IRS/GSA business travel guidance is appropriate, with higher-cost markets handled by the applicable published rate or by mutual approval in advance.
