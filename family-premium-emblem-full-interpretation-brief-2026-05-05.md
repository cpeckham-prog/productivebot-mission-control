## Exact ask
- Make the lower-center construction read more clearly **within the full emblem** without changing the emblem’s overall design family, silhouette, or letter layout.

## Locked structural read
- The emblem should read as **CEMF**.
- There should be **one of each letter only**.
- The **purple right-side form already carries the F**.
- Therefore, the lower-center orange construction must **not** create, imply, or reinforce a second `F`.

## What the lower-center area is for
- The orange lower-center structure should act as **supporting connective letter logic** inside the integrated monogram.
- Its job is to improve ownership, flow, and legibility of the existing composition.
- Its job is **not** to become a new dominant letterform.

## What must remain invariant
- Full outer silhouette of the emblem
- Crest / premium emblem character
- Integrated multi-letter monogram feel
- Existing color roles, including the purple `F` on the right
- Relative balance of navy, green, orange, and purple
- Overall composition and internal hierarchy

## What must not happen
- No duplicate `F`
- No broad re-interpretation of the lettering
- No detached orange shape design that only works in isolation
- No change that shifts the primary read away from the current CEMF structure
- No solving the local problem by creating a cleaner but different mark

## Standard for the next visual move
A valid next pass must be judged against the **full locked emblem**, not an isolated orange fragment.
It only counts as progress if it:
1. preserves the current emblem family,
2. keeps the read at CEMF,
3. does not add a second `F`, and
4. makes the lower-center orange logic feel more intentional and structurally owned.

## Working consequence
- Isolated orange-fragment iterations are not sufficient as review artifacts.
- Any future visual pass must be integrated back into the full-emblem read before it is shown as meaningful progress.
